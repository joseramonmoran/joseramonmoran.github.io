**Abstract.** The Rapid Response Labor Mechanism (RRM) introduced under the USMCA agreement in 2020 is a government-to-firm mechanism that allows the US government to delay customs processing of imports from specific manufacturing plants in Mexico if there is evidence that the manufacturing plant is violating Mexican labor law. This paper explores three main questions: (i) How do trade links and volumes change for Mexican establishments directly targeted by the RRM, both to the US and worldwide? (ii) How do establishments susceptible to the RRM change their trading behavior due to the more strict regulatory environment? And (iii) How do US firms adjust their supply networks in response to increased chances of disruption from Mexican suppliers?
